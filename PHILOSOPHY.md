# Tendril Garden — an algorithmic philosophy

## The movement: Courteous Growth

Courteous Growth is a generative aesthetic built on a single social contract between lines: every line may wander wherever it likes, but no line may ever touch another — nor its own past. The drawing is not composed; it is negotiated. Each tendril is an autonomous agent with a pen, born onto shared paper, feeling its way forward through space that is being consumed in real time by its siblings. The white of the page is a commons, and the algorithm is the etiquette that lets dozens of strangers draw on it simultaneously without a single collision. Beauty here is not placed — it emerges from restraint, meticulously enforced at every step by a spatial memory of everything ever drawn.

Growth is temporal and visible. The work is an animation of drawing itself: strokes lengthen frame by frame, the way ink is pulled across paper by a live hand. A tendril advances in small, even steps, its heading perturbed by smooth noise so the line breathes — organic, hand-drawn, never mechanical. Ahead of its pen tip it casts short feelers; when a feeler lands in claimed territory, the tendril bends away, testing angles left and right like a vine testing for light. Avoidance is not failure — it is the choreography. The negative space between lines, held open at a constant clearance, becomes as deliberate as the lines themselves.

Every tendril's life ends in one of two coils, and the coil is the signature of the movement. When a line chooses (or is forced) to stop growing outward, it turns inward and winds itself down: either as a **circular spiral** — curvature tightening continuously, radius decaying with each revolution by slightly more than the sacred clearance, so the coil nests inside itself without ever touching — or as a **triangular spiral** — straight runs broken by crisp 120-degree corners, each side shorter than the last, folding inward like a meander collapsing into itself. Round coils carry the calm of an ensō drawn in one breath; angular coils carry the geometry of the Greek key. The two vocabularies, soft and sharp, share one grammar: spiral inward, never touch, end in a resting dot of ink.

The mathematics is quiet but exact. A uniform grid — cell width equal to the clearance distance — stores every point ever inked, tagged with its owner and its age. A candidate step is legal only if no foreign point, and no sufficiently old point of the tendril's own trail, lies within the clearance radius. The spiral geometries are closed-form: the circular coil's radial decay per revolution and the triangular coil's per-side shrinkage are both derived from the clearance itself, so the non-touching guarantee is not luck but construction. Seeded randomness governs everything else — birthplaces, headings, lifespans, the choice of coil — so each seed grows a different garden, and the same seed grows the same garden forever.

This must read as the work of a master, not a demo. The wander amplitude is tuned so lines curve like plant matter rather than jitter like noise; step length is tuned against clearance so avoidance turns are smooth arcs, not panicked kinks; birth is staggered so the page fills the way frost grows across a window — first a few pioneers, then a thickening society of lines partitioning the plane between themselves. Line weight varies subtly between tendrils, ink color is a considered palette rather than random hue, and every termination — whether a wide lazy spiral in open country or a tight desperate coil in a crowded corner — must feel inevitable. The final frame should look like a painstakingly inked drawing; the path to it should look like watching that drawing being made. Every parameter is the product of deliberate calibration by someone at the top of the craft of computational drawing: nothing touches, nothing collides, and the whole page ends dense with lines that never once broke the contract.
