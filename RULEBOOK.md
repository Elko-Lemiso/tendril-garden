# Tendril Garden — the rulebook

Companion to [PHILOSOPHY.md](PHILOSOPHY.md). The philosophy says what the work *is*;
the rulebook says what the engine may and may not do, and in what order capabilities
are earned.

> Rules 1–20 predate this file and are not yet checked in — they live in the
> project's earlier discussions. Paste them above the candidate rules when available.
> The two sacred laws they orbit: **no line ever touches another**, and **every line
> resolves** — spiral, fork, or erased as if it never existed.

Tags: **[NOW]** build next · **[NEXT]** soon after · **[LATER]** earned, not scheduled ·
**[PERMANENT]** / **[ENGINEERING]** standing constraints on all work.

## Candidate rules 21–48 (added 2026-08-29)

21. **Vacancy awareness** [NOW]. Every open region has measurable properties: area,
    width, distance to ink, distance to boundary, dominant axis, connectivity, and
    local center. Lines may query this information before growing.
22. **Intentional void** [NEXT]. Empty space is allowed to become protected. Once a
    region is designated a void, filling it is not considered an improvement.
    "Available" does not mean "must be occupied."
23. **Density target** [NEXT]. A drawing seeks a configurable degree of occupation
    rather than maximum possible occupation. Different regions may carry different
    density targets.
24. **Rhythmic phrase** [NOW]. A line may remember a sequence of actions — not merely
    one previous move. A phrase can contain run lengths, turns, branch events,
    zigzags, handedness, and eventually curls.
25. **Rhythmic persistence** [NOW]. A phrase may repeat for a bounded number of cycles
    before mutating, resolving, or disappearing. Nothing repeats forever merely
    because it began repeating.
26. **Repetition with variation** [NOW]. Repeated phrases may change within controlled
    tolerances. Ornament should produce `A A′ A″ A′` as naturally as `A A A A`.
27. **Chirality** [NOW/NEXT]. Turns, spirals, curls, and phrases have handedness.
    Rhythm may preserve or alternate handedness: `L L L L`, `L R L R`, `L L R L L R`.
28. **Phrase transformation** [NEXT]. A rhythmic phrase may be translated, mirrored,
    rotated 180°, or glide-reflected before repetition. These operations should act
    on generated phrases rather than on premade motifs.
29. **Cadence / punctuation** [NEXT]. Long runs of repetition may occasionally resolve
    into a distinct event before beginning again: `A A A X A A A X`. X must itself be
    generated by the grammar. This gives ornament phrases, sentences, and endings
    rather than endless wallpaper.
30. **Proportional memory** [NEXT]. Related dimensions should preferentially reuse
    related ratios. The palette can include simple ratios such as `1:1`, `1:2`,
    `2:3`, `3:5`, and `1:φ`. The golden ratio is therefore one possible proportional
    relationship, not a universal commandment.
31. **Scale ladder** [NEXT]. Major, secondary, and minor structures occupy
    systematically related scales. Descendants generally become smaller, but line
    thickness remains constant. Large structure should be readable before small
    structure.
32. **Traveling curl** [NEXT]. A curl is not a termination. A line may turn around a
    local center, exit the curl, continue, branch, and terminate elsewhere. Terminal
    spirals remain a separate concept.
33. **Return tendency** [NEXT]. A path may be biased toward an ancestor, sibling,
    earlier trajectory, or family center without ever touching it. Return creates
    hooks, S-curves, lobes, scrolls, vines, and cloud-like structures.
34. **Behavioral equivalence** [IMPORTANT]. Do not create separate primitives for
    "Greek key," "Chinese cloud," "wave," etc. If two motifs share the same
    higher-order behavior, they should differ only through geometry. The same
    `advance → return → repeat` topology might become a square meander in 90° mode
    and a rolling scroll in flowing mode.
35. **Family inheritance** [LATER]. Descendants inherit tendencies — rhythm,
    handedness, scale, curl probability, return strength, branch interval, angle
    preference — with occasional mutation. This should create neighborhoods with
    recognizable character.
36. **Generational hierarchy** [LATER]. Branch generation affects freedom. Early
    generations organize space; later generations decorate and resolve it. Think
    `ancestor → branch → descendant → termination`, without introducing literal
    stem/leaf primitives.
37. **Alternating branching** [LATER]. Branch events may form rhythms of their own:
    `left/right/left/right`, `left/left/right`, paired siblings, etc. Palmettes,
    fern-like structures, and fans should emerge from this plus symmetry — not from a
    `drawFan()` command.
38. **Local symmetry event** [LATER]. A family may temporarily establish reflectional
    or rotational symmetry around a local axis or center. This can produce rosette-,
    star-, crown-, and palmette-like arrangements while the whole drawing remains
    asymmetric.
39. **Directional grain** [NEXT]. Growth should acknowledge the geometry of the space
    containing it. Long thin vacancies encourage longitudinal travel; edges encourage
    parallel movement; arcs encourage tangent following; corners encourage
    directional transition.
40. **Dignity** [NEXT]. Important space is not automatically filled with detail.
    Large coherent forms must be allowed enough surrounding quiet to remain legible.
41. **Crown** [LATER]. A composition may have one or a very small number of regions
    where scale, symmetry, complexity, or unusual behavior is allowed to peak. The
    focal moment should emerge from the composition rather than always being
    centered.
42. **Fence** [LATER]. High-complexity regions should tend to be bounded by quieter
    regions. Dense ornament next to dense ornament everywhere destroys hierarchy.
    Think `quiet → dense → quiet`, not uniformly busy.
43. **Recursive echo** [LATER]. Small structures may echo the topology of larger
    structures without being exact scaled copies. A major curl might contain
    descendants whose organization curls; a meandering family may generate smaller
    meandering relatives.
44. **Geometric habitat** [LATER]. Do not hard-code BORDER/CORNER/FIELD templates
    yet. Instead derive local "habitat" from vacancy geometry: elongated pocket,
    corner-like pocket, enclosed pocket, broad field, narrow channel, etc. Behavior
    probabilities can respond to those measurements.
45. **Completion at multiple scales** [LATER]. The second sacred law should
    eventually apply above the individual line. Lines finish, but phrases and
    families should also feel resolved. A repeated sequence should cadence; a family
    should terminate coherently; a compositional region should know when it is done.
46. **Never add a motif if an interaction can explain it** [PERMANENT META-RULE].
    Before adding any new primitive, try to generate the desired phenomenon from
    existing mechanisms. `rhythm + return + curl` beats `drawCloud()`.
    `rhythm + 90°` beats `drawGreekKey()`. `branching + hierarchy + return` beats
    `drawAcanthus()`.
47. **Seed isolation** [ENGINEERING]. New mechanisms should have deterministic,
    namespaced randomness so enabling rhythm or curl does not unnecessarily scramble
    unrelated legacy decisions. Same seed + same settings must remain exact; switches
    off must preserve legacy output.
48. **Every rule earns its existence** [ENGINEERING/AESTHETIC]. Every mechanism stays
    independently switchable in the harness. Compare identical seeds with and without
    it. If a rule does not create a recognizable improvement or unlock genuinely new
    expressive territory, remove it.

## The ladder of thinking

The engine stops thinking merely in terms of *lines* and starts having:

`line → gesture → phrase → repetition → cadence → family → composition`

## Build order

Not all at once. With vacancy + rhythm underway, ensure rhythm is capable of becoming
a phrase system later, then:

`vacancy → rhythm → curl → return → families → scale/composition → local symmetry → only then exotic things like interlace`

Keep the engine small enough that genuinely unexpected ornament can still emerge.

## Standing UI principles (2026-08-29)

- **Borders are made of elements, never traced.** A filled shape's silhouette emerges
  from small, quickly-resolving marks packed along the edge — never from a traced
  contour line.
- **Nothing regenerates unless the user asks.** Colors, background, and thickness are
  *paint*: they restyle the existing drawing live. Structure settings arm quietly and
  take effect on the next explicit regrow.
